§5  THE SHELF RING (A-G56 / A-G62) — THE CONCLUSION HOLDS, THE MECHANISM IS WRONG, AND min() IS CONTAMINATED TOO
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GELB credited PINK with a shelf reading PINK had not taken: §4's reply reasoned ARITHMETICALLY on GRÜN's numbers
and said so ("if that holds on my render"). A-G62 then decided a semantic change on it. So PINK measured it.

WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS, and it changes the account. PersonJobMappingField.tsx:882 draws an OPAQUE BLACK seat
(`SEAT = "rgba(0, 0, 0, 1)"`) across the FULL band width, and only then does :883 stroke the shelf in
`rgba(205, 240, 255, 0.02)` over it. **The shelf band does not sit on the plate. Its own seat occludes the plate
underneath it.** Geometry confirmed on the render: r = 465, stroke-width = 70 user units, i.e. the band spans
430…500 exactly as A-G8 has it (58.33 px at 1920, 31.08 px at 1280).

THE ARITHMETIC, which reproduces GRÜN's number exactly and identifies it:
  INK rgb(205,240,255) at alpha 0.02 over BLACK  -> rgb(4.1, 4.8, 5.1) -> rounds to **rgb(4,5,5)**, L = 1.453105e-3
  GRÜN's reported shelf engraved minimum                                                        = 0.0014531
  the plate rgb(3,5,8)                                                                     L = 1.454319e-3
  difference                                                                                   = 1.214e-6
So GRÜN is measuring the band's OWN value — ink over its own black seat — to seven significant figures.
BUT: **the two are DIFFERENT byte triples with near-equal luminance, not the same byte.** rgb(4,5,5) against
rgb(3,5,8): R differs by +1 and B by −3. A-G62's phrase "byte-identical to the plate it sits on" is wrong twice —
the bytes are not identical, and the band does not sit on the plate. The real mechanism is a COINCIDENCE OF THE
COMPOSITING CHAIN: the seat zeroes the plate under the band, and 2 % ink adds back a luminance that lands
1.2e-6 from where the plate was. A-G62's OPERATIONAL conclusion is nevertheless correct and survives intact —
on the LUMINANCE plane, which is where PF-15 is measured (A-F26/A-F31: never in sRGB alpha), 1.2e-6 is 5.6 % of
the smallest available code step (2.1915e-5, a blue +1), so no luminance instrument can separate the band from
the plate. The ring is luminance-invisible at its darkest angle. The decision stands; the reason in the record
should not.

PINK'S OWN MEASUREMENT DIVERGES FROM GRÜN'S, AND THE DIVERGENCE IS THE FINDING (reported, NOT reconciled — A-G36
point 4):

**On PINK's instrument the band's minimum across its width reaches 0 — pure black — at the darkest angle, and at
over 1 % of all angles.** That is not the band's own value; it is the OPAQUE BLACK SEAT of some mark crossing the
58 px band (shelf bodies' seats, sector edges, beams — all SEAT = rgba(0,0,0,1)).

THIS IS THE EXACT MIRROR OF PINK'S PHASE-F FINDING, IN THE OTHER DIRECTION. In phase F PINK found MAX-across-the-
band contaminated by crossing BODIES (shelf max 0.58 = a body). §29 adopted min() to fix it. **min() across the
band is contaminated by crossing SEATS.** Both extremes of a wide band crossed by marks are contaminated; the
engraved-minimum statistic inherits the same defect as the maximum it replaced, and GRÜN's clean 1.4531e-3 means
GRÜN's method is avoiding the seats by some means PINK's is not — a method difference, not a disagreement about
the build.

SO PF-15's STATISTIC NEEDS DEFECT NINETEEN'S REMEDY, NOT A CHOICE BETWEEN min AND max. GELB established the
correct technique in phase F for PF-17/PF-18 and never applied it to the ring ladder: *keep every sign drawn and
EXCLUDE sign pixels by mask* — because suppression removes medium and bare extremes read the marks. The ring's
own value is a robust central statistic over band pixels NOT covered by a mark or its seat. Until PF-15 is
measured that way, neither PINK's 0.630/0.861 nor GRÜN's 1.758/1.615 is measuring the ring.

AND A SECOND, INDEPENDENT PROBLEM WITH PF-15's LOCAL-GROUND LIMB, SPECIFIC TO THE SHELF: the band's two
neighbours are not the same ground. Just INSIDE it the plate reads 2.746e-3 (the lit `mf-ground` gradient, which
runs out to shelfNear); just OUTSIDE it 1.432e-3. That is a factor of **1.92 between the band's own two sides** —
larger than PF-15's own 1.6x threshold. "Each ring separated from its own local ground by >= 1.6x in either
direction" is therefore ILL-POSED for the shelf: which side is "its own local ground" decides the verdict, and
