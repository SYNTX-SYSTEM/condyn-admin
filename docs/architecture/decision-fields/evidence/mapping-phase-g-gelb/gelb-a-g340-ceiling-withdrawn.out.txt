[1] WHAT THE CEILING DOES AND DOES NOT BOUND

  SURVIVES — a bound on the NUMERATOR. Rounding cannot manufacture swing the opacity range
    does not contain, so s <= 0.5503 x c_true. That is physics and PINK agrees with it.
  WITHDRAWN — a bound on the RATIO as measured. c is a difference of two 8-bit luminances, so
    measured c can fall below c_true by up to one quantum and inflate s/c without limit.
  WITHDRAWN — "p95 at 1.567 and 14.59 are foreign movers, derived". They are the quantiser.
    §82's A-G372 is wrong on that sentence and the correction is PINK's, not mine.

  THE CORRECT FORM OF MY OWN TEST IS THE ONE PINK BUILT: a ceiling on s/c is a test only where
  c is resolved, i.e. stated with an admissibility rung in units of the pixel's own quantum.
  PINK's ladder IS my test, repaired. I asked for a fraction and was handed the guard my test
  was missing.

[2] A SECOND CANDIDATE FOR THE SELECTED NON-CONVERGENCE — and it is PINK's own rule
========================================================================================================

  PINK's s is `max L - min L` over frames. *** THAT IS AN EXTREME-VALUE STATISTIC. *** A-F11
  retired maxima for the measured quantity; A-G366 (PINK's) bound the ESTIMATOR; §30 applied it
  to the DIVISOR. It has not been applied to s itself, and quantisation noise inflates a
  max-minus-min by a roughly FIXED ABSOLUTE amount — so it bites small signals proportionally
  harder, which is the exact shape of the asymmetry PINK reports.

  PINK measures the median s/c at c >= 10q as 0.37582 unselected (exact 0.3756, +0.06 %)
  and 0.16919 selected (exact 0.1504, +12.5 %).

  *** MY HYPOTHESIS IS REFUTED BY MY OWN SIMULATION, AND I AM WRITING THAT HERE RATHER THAN
  RESHAPING THE SENTENCE. *** I predicted an UPWARD bias in both states, larger for selected.
  Unselected is +22.4 %, upward as predicted. Selected is -81.5 % — DOWNWARD, and not slightly.

  SO PINK'S ATTRIBUTION STANDS AND MINE IS WITHDRAWN. Residual movement remains the explanation
