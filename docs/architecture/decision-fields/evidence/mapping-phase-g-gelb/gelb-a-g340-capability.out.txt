========================================================================================================
[2] A PIXEL CAN ONLY SWING WHAT IT CARRIES — and the threshold is STATE-DEPENDENT
========================================================================================================

  A pixel's halo contribution c and its luminance swing s share the SAME gradient alpha, so the
  (S − D) term and the gradient cancel and the ratio is pure opacity:
      s / c = opSwing / op_sampled        →  capability threshold = 0.004 × op_sampled / opSwing
  which means THE THRESHOLD DEPENDS ON WHERE THE MASK WAS SAMPLED. Both variants given.

    state        opacity p-p   mask sampled at MEAN   mask sampled at PEAK
    unselected   0.23500       0.01502                0.01702
    selected     0.08225       0.04292                0.04492

    selected / unselected = 2.8571 = 1 / selectedGain, either way.

  SO NEITHER CANDIDATE IS THE THRESHOLD:
    (b) t → 1e-9    admits pixels 1.50e+7× below capability, unselected
    (a) t = 0.004   admits pixels down to 0.004 — still 3.76× below capability unselected,
                    and 10.73× below it selected

  *** DERIVED, for a mask sampled at the mean (which a `reduce` capture is, by :313):
      t = 0.004 × 0.8825 / (haloDepth × gain)  →  0.01502 unselected, 0.04292 selected. ***
  That is the set of pixels CAPABLE of the swing the limb tests. It is not a choice between two
  readings of "visible"; it is the only threshold that makes the denominator answerable by the
  numerator. (a) is in the right direction and PINK's reason for it is the right reason — it is
  simply not far enough, because the limb's threshold is a SWING and the mask's is a LEVEL.

========================================================================================================
[3] WHY (b) IS NOT MERELY PERMISSIVE — IT IS ANTI-MONOTONE IN THE THING THE PIN REWARDS
========================================================================================================

  share = (# pixels swinging ≥ 0.004) / (# pixels in mask).
  Under (b) the mask is every pixel the glow reaches. Widen the glow's reach — paint further out,
  which is the mechanism PG-1 and F-3 exist to certify — and the denominator grows while the
  numerator cannot, because the added pixels are below capability by construction.

  *** UNDER (b), A STRICTLY BETTER GLOW READS WORSE. *** The pin punishes the mechanism it certifies.
  That is not a bias to be corrected; it is the wrong sign on the derivative, and it is why this
  is a contract question and not a tolerance question.

  (a) and the derived form have the OPPOSITE failure mode, and it must be guarded: shrink the glow
  to a tiny bright core and the mask shrinks with it, so the share → 1. PINK named this exact
  artefact for blur — "a mask that keeps only the bright core is where spreading always wins".
  RULED WITH THE THRESHOLD: the mask POPULATION is reported beside every share. A share without
  its denominator cannot be audited for this, and A-G340's four numbers did not carry one.

========================================================================================================
[4] PINK'S SWEEP, RE-READ — the selected column is PF-10's compliance, not the glow's liveness
========================================================================================================

  FIRST, THE DISCRIMINATOR I NEARLY USED, AND WHY IT IS THE WRONG WAY ROUND. I was going to write
  "if this were an independent observation, sel/none would vary with the rung". The arithmetic says
  the opposite: with ONE mask M shared by both states, share = N/M, so sel/none = N_sel/N_none and
  M cancels — the ratio is RUNG-INDEPENDENT by construction. And every pixel capable of the swing
  is already inside the t = 0.004 mask (capability 0.01502 > 0.004), so dropping t adds only
  incapable pixels, which change M and not N. The ratio should be FLAT.

    rung                 1920 sel/none   1280 sel/none
    old whole-clip          0.2943          0.2074
    t = 0.004               0.3487          0.2843
    t = 0.002               0.3643          0.3076
    t = 0.001               0.3791          0.3342
    disc floor → 1e-9       0.3953          0.3283

    mean 0.3244   sd 0.0518   span 0.2074 … 0.3953
    WAVE.selectedGain = 0.35          |mean − selectedGain| = 0.0256

  IT IS NOT FLAT. Over the four t rungs it drifts 0.0466 at 1920 and 0.0440 at 1280, both upward
  as t falls. *** THAT IS A SECOND FINDING AND IT IS ABOUT THE MASK, NOT THE GLOW: *** a shared,
  state-independent mask cannot produce a t-dependent ratio. Either the mask is built PER STATE,
  or the 2 px blur is mixing pixels across the mask boundary so that changing M changes the
  measured swings too. Both are checkable on PINK's stand and neither is visible in the shares.

  WHAT SURVIVES THE CORRECTION, AND IT IS THE POINT: ten cells, five rungs, two viewports, and the
  ratio sits at mean 0.3244, sd 0.0518 — 7.3 % from the declared selectedGain of 0.35. A
  share measuring "does a quarter of the glow breathe" has no reason to land on a CSS constant.
  To first order the selected column is selectedGain re-measured; the drift is the residual.

  AND PF-10 REQUIRES IT. PF-10: "with a capability selected, the measured breath amplitude is
  ≤ 45 % of its unselected value." selectedGain = 0.35 ≤ 0.45, satisfied with margin.

  *** SO LIMB 1'S SELECTED READING PENALISES THE BUILD FOR COMPLYING WITH PF-10. *** A fixed
  absolute swing threshold against a deliberately reduced amplitude is a pin fighting a pin.
  "Selected fails 10 of 10" is not a defect in the build; it is the limb measuring the wrong
  quantity in that state.
